See what your quantum program is doing.

Q-VTK brings together visualization techniques for every layer of quantum computing: quantum states, circuits, noisy hardware and quantum neural networks. Each module is built on a published, expert-evaluated visualization design.

Four layers, one toolkit

Quantum programs are hard to reason about because the things that matter cannot be printed: amplitudes, entanglement, gate effects, device noise and trained parameters. Q-VTK gives each of these a visual form.

The modules

Each module comes from a peer-reviewed paper. The tag on each card names the layer it serves and the paper it is built on.

VENUS

STATES · EuroVis 2023

A 2D geometric view of quantum states that shows entanglement and superposition.

The Bloch sphere cannot show entanglement and can hide details through 3D occlusion. VENUS maps quantum amplitudes to 2D shapes grounded in the math of quantum computing, for single qubits and entangled pairs, and uses coordinated semicircles to show the probability distribution.

Evaluated with two case studies and an expert interview.

QuantumEyes

CIRCUITS · IEEE TVCG 2024

Explains what a quantum circuit does, gate by gate.

At the global level, a Probability Summary View, a State Evolution View and a Gate Explanation View trace how gates change the quantum state. At the local level, the Dandelion Chart shows how amplitudes determine the probability of each basis state.

Evaluated with two case studies and interviews with 12 domain experts.

VACSEN

NOISE · IEEE VIS 2022 / TVCG

Noise-aware choice of quantum computer and compiled circuit.

Linked views track each machine's performance metrics over time, filter compiled circuits to the most promising ones, and compare qubit and gate usage. Fidelity and probability-distribution views show the expected result quality, so users can pick the setup with the highest fidelity instead of trial and error.

Evaluated with two case studies on quantum algorithms of different scales.

Basis-State Views

NOISE · QCCC 2023

Compares output distributions across quantum computers and simulators.

For the circuits of a quantum machine learning application, this module plots the distribution of every basis state on several quantum computers and on noise-free simulators, and draws a heat map of the KL distance between states. Users see at a glance where real hardware departs from the ideal result.

Built with Oak Ridge National Laboratory.

NoiseLens

NOISE · IEEE QSW 2025

Separates noisy from less noisy basis states automatically.

A four-step pipeline: KL divergence between every pair of basis states, a 2D map by multidimensional scaling, k-means clustering, and violin plots that flag high-variance clusters as noisy. Demonstrated on a 7-qubit MNIST classifier with about 8,000 circuit runs on IBM Lagos, Nairobi and Perth, with far less clutter than functional box plots.

Built with Oak Ridge National Laboratory. Source code on GitHub

VIOLET

QML · IEEE TVCG 2024

Opens the black box of quantum neural networks.

Coordinated views explain the three quantum-specific parts of a QNN: how input data is encoded into quantum states, how the ansatz evolves during training, and what the final measurements mean. It introduced new visual designs, including an augmented heat map, and was the first visualization work aimed at explainable quantum machine learning.

Evaluated with two case studies and interviews with 12 domain experts.

Encoder Explorer

QML · PacificVis 2026 / TVCG

Helps choose a data encoder for a QNN before training.

Picking a quantum data encoder is usually trial and error, because encoded states are hard to evaluate before training and there are few intuitive ways to compare encoders. This module visualizes the encoded quantum states so users can compare encoders and make an informed choice up front.

A step toward explainable quantum AI.

Field guide

SURVEY

Where Q-VTK sits in the wider field.

Our survey, Visualization for Quantum Computing: Current State and Future Directions, reviews existing visualization approaches for quantum computing and lays out open problems. It shapes the roadmap for new Q-VTK modules.
